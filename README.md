## Plan a small pilot

1. Write one sentence describing what the audience must understand. Pick the subject, visible action, setting and camera move using [the scene brief](templates/scene-brief.txt).
2. Decide your acceptance checks before generating. If dialogue matters, check the exact words and performance; if an object matters, check its shape and interaction.
3. Generate one pilot, then inspect its beginning, middle, end and moving frames. Use [the shot review](templates/shot-review.txt). A pleasing still does not establish a usable moving shot.
4. Keep approved picture and sound separately in your edit. If just one element fails, first consider an editing repair or a smaller replacement shot. Another full generation may change elements you already liked.
5. Count creative rejects in your consumption cost. Keep pack cash outlay, net credits consumed and approved usable seconds separate. A confirmed technical failure that restores its credit differs from a delivered shot you decide to discard.

For recurring fictional characters, keep a fixed text description and restate each scene completely. Review face, wardrobe and action separately. [Our actual character-test guide](https://seedancecheap.com/consistent-characters?utm_source=github&utm_medium=cookbook&utm_campaign=creator_value_oct06&utm_content=cookbook_character_guide) shows why consistent clothing can coexist with facial drift.
