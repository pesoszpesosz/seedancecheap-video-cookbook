1. Inspect the original brief

A cinematic pearl-white concept sports car glides through a rain-soaked neon city at night. The camera tracks beside it, then arcs toward the front as electric blue and amber lights ripple across wet pavement. Photorealistic reflections, elegant motion, no logos, no people, no text.

The brief combines a moving subject, a changing viewpoint and moving reflections. Review those three demands separately. A pleasing neon color palette does not establish that the car stays intact through the arc.

Geometry

Follow the same wheels, doors, windows and body proportions as the camera moves. Note the time of any visible change.

Contact and light

Check tire contact, road direction and reflected light through motion. Watch for abrupt changes around the car.

Camera continuity

Watch the side-to-front transition at normal speed. Beginning, middle and end stills alone cannot verify the movement between them.

2. Plan one shot you can judge

Start with one fictional vehicle and one main action. Keep the camera fixed if you want to isolate the subject motion; try a tracking arc only when that viewpoint change is part of the brief.
