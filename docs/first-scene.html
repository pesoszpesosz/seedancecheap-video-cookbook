1. Read a real, simple prompt

This is the original prompt for our existing paper-boat example. Its output is a historical sample; a new attempt can differ.

A small paper boat glides across a calm lake at dawn, sunlight reflects on the water, gentle camera dolly, coherent motion, no text.

Watch the complete paper-boat result and inspect its prompt →

Subject

A small paper boat.

Action

It glides across water.

Setting

A calm lake at dawn.

Camera

A gentle dolly move.

Light

Sunlight reflecting on the water.

The prompt describes the intended motion. Watch moving frames to judge whether the boat shape, water contact and camera movement actually work.

2. Plan your version before generating

For an object-only scene, start with the prompt structure above and the notes below. For a fictional-character scene, the character builder helps organize the character sheet and scene instructions together.

  1. Write one sentence the audience should understand. Start with one subject and one main action.
  2. Describe the place, light and camera. Keep the first camera instruction simple enough to judge.
  3. Choose two or three checks for success: an object keeps its shape, the action reads, or the camera follows the intended path.
  4. If you use a fictional adult character, keep the same text description when you change the scene. Review face, clothing and action separately; a text sheet cannot guarantee an identical face.

Edits stay in this page until you download them. No account, uploads or automatic saving. The linked text prompt builder helps turn your character and scene details into a prompt to review.

3. Generate a test, then review the whole clip

Before the request

  • Open your chosen tool’s text-to-video workflow and review the prompt and request settings.
  • Check availability, price, request length and output format. A short finished edit can still require a longer generation request.
  • Plan an original scene and use reference images you have permission to use. Your chosen video engine’s content restrictions still apply.
  • For SeedanceCheap, face/person reference images and input-video continuation are unsupported. Fictional people can be described in text.

After the result arrives

  • Play the complete result. Inspect the beginning, middle, end and the motion between them.
  • Check subject shape, physical contact and camera movement against your brief. Review sound separately.
  • If a specific instruction failed, change that instruction for the next test. Count delivered creative rejects in your budget.

A job still processing is not a delivered clip. Save your prompt and keep the existing request ID while you wait; a page delay alone is not a reason to submit the same request again.

If the request is refused

Read the reason shown on the existing job and review your scene and references before deciding on a revised request. SeedanceCheap leaves prompt and reference refusals for you to review instead of automatically generating again. If a free job ends as Failed, its trial entitlement becomes available again, subject to eligibility checks and at most three attempts per 24-hour window. Check the current entitlement or credits before confirming another request.

When the file is Ready

Download the MP4 before closing out your review. SeedanceCheap holds a delivered private file for 24 hours from delivery; save your copy while that window is open.

4. Finish the scene in your editor

Keep the parts that communicate your intended action. Add final captions, a readable website credit and your sound mix in the edit. Inspect the final file’s picture and audio before uploading it.

Keep the original footage and project. Record source dimensions and delivery dimensions separately. A larger export canvas does not establish higher native source detail.

Finalize your media before upload and keep that published or scheduled copy. You can improve titles, descriptions and comments later.
