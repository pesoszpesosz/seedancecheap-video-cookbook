Plan the chorus before you animate it.

Start with 12–15 seconds of your own recording and three clear visual moments. Make a still animatic, then decide which movement is worth generating.

1. Pick the smallest useful pilot

  1. Choose the exact start and end in audio you own or have permission to use. Write the lyric or emotional turn this excerpt needs to convey.
  2. Mark three visual beats on that recording. Their lengths should follow your actual phrasing; the equal four-second blocks below are only an example.
  3. Place sketches, placeholders or approved stills on those timings in your editor. Play them with the original recording. Keep the original vocals and mix on a separate audio track.
  4. Watch the still animatic without sound once. Check whether its intended story reads. Then watch with sound to check the timing and mood.
  5. Choose the moments that need movement. A deliberate held image can be part of the edit. Agree on framing, revisions and a spending cap before commissioning the full song.

2. Give each beat one visible job

Hypothetical 12-second excerpt: a fictional adult returns to a café, finds a map, then chooses to leave. This is a proposed plan, not generated footage or a tested song.

0–4 seconds · establish

The empty chair

A fictional adult in a navy coat and rust-red scarf enters a small café at sunrise. An empty chair sits beside the window.

Check: the entrance, chair and performer are readable in the chosen crop.

4–8 seconds · discover

The notebook

The adult opens a blue notebook on the table. A handwritten map is inside. Keep the camera move simple.

Check: the notebook and hand interaction stay coherent; the map need not contain legible generated text.

8–12 seconds · resolve

The choice

The adult closes the notebook, takes it and exits. The camera holds on the empty chair.

Check: the exit direction and final image communicate the intended ending.

3. Write the plan before the prompt

No uploads, analytics or automatic saving. Download your edits before closing the page. No song, recording or picture is sent to SeedanceCheap from this worksheet.

4. Judge the pilot in the actual edit

Review picture and sound separately

  • Check the intended action in moving frames, not just a thumbnail.
  • Compare face, wardrobe, props and location separately between cuts.
  • Review any lip-sync against the original vocal timing. Test a short portion before relying on it for an entire song.
  • Keep approved picture and audio; repair the smallest failed section when your editor permits.

Budget for what survives

  • Count paid creative rejects in the production budget.
  • Record unique seconds kept in the edit, plus editing and any licensed media costs.
  • A 12-second finished pilot is not necessarily a 12-second generation request. Check the tool’s request length and editing options.
  • For SeedanceCheap, a credit covers a 30-second request. Face/person reference images and input video are unsupported; text descriptions can drift.
