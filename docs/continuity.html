A small handoff to plan

This doorway example is a proposed shot plan, not generated footage.

Shot A · reach

A hand reaches a brass door handle. End with the hand resting on it, the sleeve visible, and the handle still level.

Shot B · open

Begin with that same hand, sleeve and handle position. The hand turns the handle and the door opens into the room.

Review the hand, handle and direction at the actual cut. If the join is rough, an insert of the hinge or room detail may bridge it. A continuous room or rain track can help the sound feel connected.

1. Keep the shared details unchanged

Reuse the same concise character or subject description. Record wardrobe, props, location, light and movement direction separately from each shot's action.

Check the controls your actual tool exposes. Google documents reference-image and first/last-frame controls for Veo 3.1, with model and person-generation limits; an interface may expose a different subset. Read Google's current Veo documentation →

2. Write the end-to-start handoff

Give each shot one main action and one clear camera instruction. Match the relevant pose, prop state and direction at the handoff. Use each tool's supported request length.

If your generator supports a starting image, a clean final frame from A can guide B's start. Inspect it first: a distorted frame can carry the defect forward. Reusing a frame or description still requires output review.

3. Review the moving pair in your editor

  • Compare character appearance, wardrobe, props, lighting and screen direction across the cut.
  • Watch the complete moving shots. Check hands, contact, geometry and any changing details.
  • Review dialogue, voice and background sound separately. Keep useful sound continuous where it fits.
  • Decide whether to keep the pair, trim the cut, use an insert, revise a shot, or stop. Count creative rejects in your budget.

Cut from the look to the clue

Nico raises a red toy train. In one cut you see the train first. In the other, Maya looks down and you follow her eyes to it. Both cuts end on the same reaction.

Try the two orders

  1. Look: Crop to Maya’s face from 42.00 to 43.50 seconds. Keep her eyes in view and leave the train out of frame.
  2. Clue: Use 44.00 to 45.25 seconds. Frame the red train and the hand that holds it.
  3. Reaction: Use 46.75 to 48.50 seconds. Go wider so her look back at Nico connects the two people.

Build clue → look → reaction and look → clue → reaction without changing the clips or sound between versions. Play both for someone who hasn’t seen the film and ask what they noticed first.

Show the contact before the reveal

Build the suspense in three beats

  1. 0:00–0:01. Start tight enough to see the moving wheel and the hand. Match the sharp latch sound to the contact at 0.53 seconds. Let the wheel stop in the same frame.
  2. 0:01–0:03. Pull back with the line still in view. Keep both ends connected on screen as the lantern and the river come into frame.
  3. 0:03–0:06. Let the crest pass under the lantern. Give the hanging line time to swing back so the viewer can see what held.

Try the opening on mute and ask someone what stopped moving. Then play with sound and check whether they hear the latch at the moment they see it close.
